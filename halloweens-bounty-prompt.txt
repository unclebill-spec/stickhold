HALLOWEENS BOUNTY

One complete build prompt. This is the Halloween game, not a Warcraft game with new paint. Anywhere a Warcraft rule, unit, building, item, or boss was discussed and then changed, only the changed Halloween version is written here. The old names are not units, not heroes, and not buttons. The detail of those rules is kept.

It is a small real-time strategy game for one player against one enemy. One map. Two resources. One hero each. A match should last about eight to twelve minutes. It must play on a phone and on a computer. The player gathers, builds, trains, takes creep camps, levels a hero, and wins by destroying the enemy town hall.

Do not make, name, or show: Humans, Orcs, Night Elves, Naga, blood elves, a Paladin, a Blademaster, a Death Knight as someone the player hires, footmen, grunts, riflemen, raiders, crypt fiends, priests of the light, shamans of a clan, ghouls as a player unit, or any other Warcraft character. Ghouls, ghosts, witches, vampires, and the Headless Horseman exist only as the Halloween creatures described below.


============================================================
1. TITLE SCREEN
============================================================

The first screen is a painting, not a form on a blank page.

A huge Headless Horseman on a black horse, in moonlight. He holds his flaming pumpkin head out in one hand. That flame is the brightest light in the picture. It lights his coat, the horse's neck, the bit, and the pumpkins around the hooves. The horse is in profile or three-quarter, large enough that the rider is not a speck. Behind him: one stone crypt with a dark door, dead trees, and a mix of graves. Fog lies on the ground. The sky is blue-black with one pale moon. No bright green grass. No daylight.

The title is Halloweens Bounty. Large, readable, candle-gold. One button under it: Begin.

Begin does not start the match. It opens setup on top of the same painting.

Setup:
- Your faction. Four choices: Vampires, Ghosts, Werewolves, Zombies and Mummies.
- Enemy faction. The same four. The enemy may be the same faction as you.
- Enemy mood. Passive, Normal, or Aggressive.
- Under your faction, one sentence that says what that faction does.
- Under the mood, one sentence that says what that mood does.
- Button: Start the night.

There is no campaign select, no difficulty ladder beyond the mood, no portrait, and no lore page.


============================================================
2. HOW THE WORLD IS DRAWN
============================================================

Simple 3D isometric. One tilted ground plane. The camera looks down at a fixed angle and never rolls, pitches, or becomes first person. Models are low and chunky, readable at phone size. Buildings stay small enough that an army in front of them still reads. Units are solid figures with a head, a body, and a weapon. They are not stick drawings and they are not realistic people. No cutscenes. No talking heads. No unit portraits in the interface.

Night for the whole match. There is no day, no dusk cycle, and no clock that changes the sky. Moonlight comes from one side, cool and pale. Warm light comes only from fire: the Horseman's pumpkin, tower torches, crypt cracks, candles on the altar, and the Pumpkin King's head.

A thin fog sits on the ground. It is thicker beside crypts, graves, and camps. It is atmosphere. It is not a black unexplored map. The whole field can be seen by panning. Do not hide the map behind fog of war.

Colors stay dull except for fire, eyes, blood, and magic. The palette:

- Night sky and shadows: #0c1218
- Panel and stone: #141c28 and #2a313c
- Moonlight on cloth and fog: #d5e4f2
- Mist and ghost edges: #9ec4dc
- Candle and gold glints: #f2c14d
- Pumpkin flesh: #e07a2f
- Blood and vampire cloth: #9a3040
- Sick green for mummies and zombies: #6f8f62
- Wolf fur: #b08a5a
- Dead grass: #243028 and #3d4a44
- Bark: #1a120e
- Bone and old cloth highlights: #d7c4a8
- Creep bodies, so they are not a faction: #8d9aa8

Health bars are hidden at full health. When something is hurt, a short bar floats over it. Your bars are dull green (#7dcea0). Enemy and creep bars are blood red (#9a3040). A hero also shows a small level number over its head all the time, even at full health.

Hits throw a short number that floats up and fades. Ordinary damage is candle-gold. Magic damage is pale purple (#c9a0e8). Words may float the same way: mist, miss, hex, seal, tithe, wrap, prowl, savage.

A selected unit gets a thin gold ring on the ground under its feet. A selected building gets a thin gold rectangle. Do not put a giant box around the screen.

Faction body colors, so two armies never blend:

- Vampires: deep blood red #9a3040, pale pink faces #f3d5da.
- Ghosts: pale moon blue #9ec4dc, dark ink shadows #10202c. They are slightly transparent.
- Werewolves: muddy brown #b08a5a, darker fur, near-black noses and claws.
- Zombies and mummies: sick green #6f8f62, cloth and bone in #d7c4a8.


============================================================
3. TEXTURES AND MATERIALS
============================================================

Keep every surface simple, but each material must be different at a glance. No shiny plastic. No clean marble kingdoms. No steel plate on soldiers.

Ground
- Dark packed dirt with a few dead-grass tufts. The grass is blue-green and dry, not lawn green.
- Near graves the dirt is darker and a little wet, with a dull highlight on one edge so it looks damp under the moon.
- No cobble roads. No bright paths.

Stone
- Crypts, graves, the altar, and gold rock share a blue-gray stone (#3a4250 to #6d7582).
- Crypt stone is larger blocks with one dark door slot and one warm crack of fire.
- Grave stone is smaller and more worn. The four grave shapes must not share one silhouette.
- Gold rock is the same dark stone with candle-gold specks in the cracks, not a pile of coins.

Wood and trees
- Bark is near-black brown with a few vertical cuts.
- The canopy is a dull dead green, almost gray. A normal tree has no face.
- A sleeper tree is the same, plus two dark cuts that read as eyes only when you are close. Do not put a cartoon face on every tree.
- Chopped trees leave nothing, or a low stump that does not block walking. Stumps are not on the map at the start.
- Pumpkin patches use a short wooden fence, rough, not painted white.

Pumpkins
- Round, slightly flattened, ribbed. Orange #e07a2f. A dark green-brown stem.
- The Horseman's pumpkin and the King's head are cut like lanterns: jagged mouth, inner fire #f2c14d. Not a smile.

Cloth
- Vampire coats are long, matte, deep red, torn at the hem.
- Ghost bodies are gauze. You can see the ground through the edge.
- Mummy wraps are dirty linen, layered, with gaps of gray-green skin.
- Wolf cloaks are hide, same brown as the fur, with a pale moon mark scratched in, not embroidered in gold thread.

Bone, metal, fire
- Bone is dull, not white plastic. Skulls on the altar, the shield, and the mummy staff are the same bone color.
- The only bright metal is a short silver-gray edge on the vampire knight's sword and the barracks wall icon. Everything else is rust, brass, or wood.
- Fire is a small teardrop of yellow inside orange. Use it sparingly: torches, candles, two pumpkin heads, crypt cracks.

Fur and skin
- Wolves have short chunky fur, not hair cards. Ears and a muzzle must read at icon size.
- Vampire skin is pale. Zombie skin is gray-green and patchy. Ghosts have no solid skin, only a lighter face in the gauze.


============================================================
4. THE LAND
============================================================

One map, about 42 by 42 tiles. Two bases in opposite corners. Each corner is cleared of trees, graves, and creeps for several tiles. Each player starts with a finished town hall, four workers, and a gold mine beside the hall.

Starting gold is 320. Starting lumber is 220.

Gold
- Two mines, one near each hall. A mine is dark rock with gold specks, two tiles wide.
- A worker stands in front of the rock, off to the side, never hidden behind it.
- Each swing adds about 8 gold to what they carry. They carry home at about 12. They drop it at the hall, then walk back to the same mine until told otherwise.
- The mine does not run out in a normal match.

Lumber
- Dead trees. A normal tree holds about 40 wood. Each swing takes about 10 and adds it to the load. At 0 the tree is gone and walking opens through that tile.
- Workers stand in front of the trunk, to one side, never behind it.
- A few trees are sleepers. Chopping a normal tree does not wake it. Walking onto a sleeper wakes it: the tree tile disappears, and a Waking Tree creep stands there. It is slow, it slams in a short circle, it slows what it hits, and it does not chase across the map.

Pumpkins
- Decoration only. Not a resource. Not food by themselves. Food is the pumpkin-patch building.
- They spawn in patches of several, not as single lonely pumpkins. A few patches sit nearer the bases so the start looks planted. More sit in the wild.

Crypts
- Small stone houses. Dark door. One lit crack. They do not train, do not shop, and do not block a huge area. They mark the wild and break up the ground. Scatter several, not on top of the halls.

Graves
- Four shapes, mixed through the middle of the map, never only one kind:
  - Traditional slab. A low rectangle with a curved top.
  - Cross. A stone cross on a short base.
  - Statue. A small standing figure, worn, no face detail beyond a lump head.
  - Pillar. A tall narrow post, taller than a slab, shorter than a tree.
- They block a little walking space. They are not gold and not wood.

Camps
- Placed between the bases, not on the starting clearings.
- A camp is a few creeps with a shared home point.
- One hit on any member wakes the whole camp. They attack the attacker.
- If the attacker leaves, they walk home and stop. They do not respawn.
- One grove of trees is already awake and counts as a camp. Other waking trees stay disguised until walked on.

The ground is flat for walking. Do not add cliffs that trap units. Buildings, graves, crypts, trees, and rocks block. Units path around them. Two buildings side by side must leave a gap a worker can walk through. Units must not stick on corners.


============================================================
5. FACTIONS, TROOPS, AND HOW THEY LOOK
============================================================

Four factions. Each has the same jobs: one worker, one melee, one ranged, one caster, and one hero. Draw them as a row of five, left to right, in that order. The hero is clearly larger and has a bigger weapon. No armor on anyone. Weapons, cloth, fur, and wraps are the costume.

Vampires are fragile. They have less health than the same job in another faction. Each hit they land gives a little blood back to the attacker.
- Thrall, worker. Thin, pale, torn shirt, a sack or a pick. No sword.
- Knight, melee. Long matte red coat, one straight silver-gray sword, red eyes. No shield.
- Crossbow, ranged. Cloak, wooden crossbow, a short quiver of pale bolts.
- Blood priest, caster. Robes, a small chalice. A faint red mist at the hands while casting.
- Vampire Lord, hero. Taller. Longer coat, high collar, heavier sword. Once his aura is learned, a thin red thread of light stays around him.

Ghosts are a little faster. They pass through their own units, so a ghost army does not jam on itself. They do not pass through enemies, trees, or buildings.
- Shade, worker. Half-there, soft edges, a dim lantern.
- Specter, melee. A ragged sheet of light and a short blade of pale fire.
- Banshee, ranged. Long hair, open mouth. The shot is a wisp, not a wooden arrow.
- Haunt, caster. Priest-shaped gauze, a censer. A green-blue cold at the feet while casting.
- Ghost Lord, hero. Larger, a crown of mist, a long pale weapon. When he Fades he is barely there.

Werewolves move faster and hit harder, and their soldiers cost more. Workers are not the expensive ones.
- Packhand, worker. Hunched, still mostly a person, fur on the arms, hauling wood or a sack of ore.
- Brute, melee. A standing wolf-man, thick arms, claws. No armor.
- Raider, ranged. Leaner, a bone spear or a thrown javelin.
- Moon shaman, caster. Hide cloak, a moon scratched on the chest, pale dust when casting.
- Alpha, hero. The biggest wolf. Moon Prowl turns him smoky. Two Shadows peels two smaller copies off him. The owner can still see him while he is smoked.

Zombies and mummies are cheaper and weaker, except the hero, who is not discounted. The lord's aura and coil are what make the crowd worth it.
- Zombie, worker. Slow, gray-green, a shovel or a bundle of wraps.
- Zombie soldier, melee. A fresher corpse, a club or a broken sword. No armor.
- Mummy archer, ranged. Bandages, one arm free, a short bow, cloth-wrapped arrows.
- Mummy priest, caster. Heavy bandages, a staff with a small skull.
- Mummy Lord, hero. Tall, a tall staff, a crown of bone. Rise Again pulls smaller zombies out of the ground at his feet.

Creep shapes, none of them player units, are in the camp section. They use the gray-blue body color unless a boss color is called out.


============================================================
6. WHAT MOVEMENT AND MAGIC LOOK LIKE
============================================================

Effects stay small enough to read on a phone. One trick per action. No screen-filling storms except the Alpha's spin, and even that stays around his body.

Shared
- Walk: a short bob. Attack: the weapon swings, or the bow fires, or the claws cut. Death: the body drops. If it is your soldier or the enemy's soldier, and not a hero and not a creep, it stays as a corpse for a short time. Corpses are what Night Rising uses. Heroes do not leave a usable corpse. Creeps do not leave a usable corpse.
- A building under construction shows one green bar filling over it. A unit in the training queue shows the time over that building. Do not stack two bars on the same spot. If the building is hurt, the bar over it is red and shows health instead, not a second bar beside the build bar.
- A hurt building may grow a small orange flame as it loses health, a little larger at each fifth of its health. The flame sits by the door or the roof edge, not over the whole sprite.

Vampires
- A landed hit flicks a red drop.
- Blood Tithe is a red thread from the lord to the ally or the enemy.
- Mist Form is a gold-red outline around the lord. Hits on him while it lasts show the word mist and do nothing.
- Night Rising is two bodies pulling up out of the ground.

Ghosts
- The body is see-through at the edges all the time.
- Wail is a pale ring on one enemy.
- Fade drops the body to a fainter alpha. Misses show the word miss.
- Procession raises two short-lived specters that fade out, they do not fall as corpses.

Werewolves
- Footfalls read heavier, a deeper bob.
- Savage Strike flashes gold on the hit that doubles.
- Moon Prowl is smoke. The next hit is the big one, then the smoke ends.
- Wolfstorm is a spin and a short arc of claws.
- The two shadows are smaller copies, paler, and they fade instead of leaving corpses.

Zombies and mummies
- Wrapping Coil is a bandage of gold-brown light, either into an ally or onto an enemy.
- Consume pulls a nearby zombie into the lord. The lord's health jumps. The zombie is gone.
- Rot Aura is a faint green trail on your own dead while they move, only after it is learned.
- Rise Again raises three short-lived zombies.

Bosses
- The Pumpkin King's smash shakes the screen once, a small shake, not a long one.
- His thrown pumpkin is a flaming ball that travels and bursts.
- The Horseman's charge is a fast slide with fire trailing the pumpkin.
- The witch's hex floats the word hex in purple.
- A waking tree's slam is a ring of roots on the ground.
- The werewolf's howl is a short pale ring around him, not a lightning bolt.


============================================================
7. BUILDINGS
============================================================

Every faction uses the same six buildings. The shell is dark stone and a near-black roof. A band of the faction color runs under the roof. A faction mark sits on the side, not instead of the door. Doors face the camera. Workers stand in front, off to the side.

The roof is a solid pyramid. No gaps you can see the sky through. The peak is the high point. A small faction flag or banner stands off to the side of the hall, shaped like a short cross with a rectangular cloth hanging from it. On other buildings the faction color is the band and the icon, not a second flag on every roof.

Town hall
- The biggest footprint, about three tiles by three. About 900 health. Already built at the start.
- Trains workers.
- Losing your last town hall loses the game.
- It can be raised once. Raising costs 200 gold and 150 lumber. The raise adds a smaller second tier on the roof. It adds a little population cap. It does not unlock a new unit.
- Look: a door, a short step, a pyramid roof. Vampires hang dark red cloth and set a blood bowl by the door. Ghosts have pale curtains that shift. Werewolves hang hides and scratch a moon by the door. Mummies wrap the door posts and set bone finials. The banner stands beside the building, not in the doorway.

Pumpkin patch
- This is the farm. About two by two. 40 gold, 70 lumber, about 16 seconds, about 280 health.
- Raises the unit cap by 8. Does not train.
- Look: a rough fence and a patch of pumpkins. A small green bar while it is built.

Barracks
- Two by two. 150 gold, 80 lumber, about 24 seconds, about 520 health.
- Trains the melee soldier.
- Look: a low hall. On the side, a sideways silver sword. No shield icon.

Lodge
- Two by two. 140 gold, 90 lumber, about 24 seconds, about 460 health.
- Trains the ranged unit and the caster.
- Look: a darker hut, same pyramid roof. On the side, a bow with an arrow beside it.

Altar
- Two by two. 180 gold, 70 lumber, about 26 seconds, about 480 health.
- Hires the one hero. Revives that hero if the hero is dead. Revival costs 160 gold and 40 lumber, takes time, and keeps the hero's level and items.
- Look: a stone table, candles, one skull. Fire on the candles.

Tower
- Two by two. 100 gold, 50 lumber, about 18 seconds, about 420 health.
- Shoots enemies that walk into range. The shot is an arrow or a small bolt in the faction color, not a round cannonball.
- Look: taller than the hut, about two stories, an open top, a torch in the middle of the roof, a merlon at each of the four corners. The torch is yellow fire, not a blue crystal. This game has no mage-tower upgrade.

A selected finished building can set a rally by tapping the ground. New units walk there. If the rally is on a tree or a mine, a new worker starts gathering. If the rally is on an enemy and the new unit can fight, it attacks when it arrives.

There is no blacksmith, no stables, no mage school, no shop building the player builds, and no second hall required. The only seller is the neutral peddler in the item section.


============================================================
8. CONTROLS
============================================================

The controls match Stickhold. Mouse and finger both have to work. Panning must not also issue an order.

Computer
- W, A, S, D and the arrow keys pan.
- The mouse wheel zooms in and out. Q and E may zoom too. Keep zoom in a small range so the angle never flips.
- Escape opens pause. Escape again, or a resume button, closes it.
- Drag a box with the mouse to select units. The box does not select buildings.
- Double-click a unit to select every unit of that type currently on screen.
- Tab cycles through units of the selected type anywhere on the map, and the camera moves to the one it lands on.
- Control plus any number key saves the current selection as that group. Pressing the number again selects the group. A unit may be in more than one group. Do not limit groups to 1 through 4.
- Click a unit to select it. Click your own unit while others are selected if you meant to pick that one, before a click on the ground moves everyone.
- Click the ground to move. Click an enemy to attack. Click a tree to chop. Click a gold mine to mine.
- With workers selected, choose a building button. The next ground tap places that building, unless Stop was pressed first. Stop cancels placement.
- With a finished building selected, the next ground tap sets the rally, as described above.

Phone
- One finger sliding pans the camera.
- A pinch opens or closes to zoom.
- Tap and hold, then drag, draws the selection box. A short tap does not draw a box.
- A short tap selects one unit or gives the order.
- Double-tap a unit to select every unit of that type on screen.
- Building placement, rally, gather, and attack use the same taps as on a computer.
- If the finger moved enough to pan, do not also give an order when it lifts.

A small map sits in a corner. It may be dragged so it does not cover a button. Tap it to center the camera there. It shows trees, gold, crypts, and both halls in faction or team colors.

The command bar sits at the bottom, in two rows when there are many buttons, so it does not eat the field.
- It names the selection. A hero's name includes the level.
- Stop is always there. Stop clears the order. On a building, each press of Stop removes one unit from the training queue.
- Workers get the five build buttons with prices.
- A finished hall gets Train worker and Raise hall.
- A finished barracks gets the melee button.
- A finished lodge gets ranged and caster.
- A finished altar gets Hire hero and Revive hero.
- A hero gets four ability buttons, each showing rank and cooldown, plus the items currently carried.
- Empty selection says to tap a unit, then the ground, and that a drag looks around.

Pause freezes the match.
- Game speed: slow, normal, fast.
- Music volume.
- Effect volume.
- Resume.

Buttons darken and press in for a moment when used, so a tap is visible.


============================================================
9. MATCH, MOODS, AND THE ENEMY
============================================================

You win when the enemy town hall is destroyed. You lose when yours is. One enemy. No allies.

Population
- The town hall gives 10 cap. Raising it adds 4.
- Each finished pumpkin patch adds 8.
- A hero costs 2. Every other unit costs 1. Units still in the queue count.
- You cannot start a unit that would pass the cap. The line is: Build another pumpkin patch. You are at the unit cap.

Moods, chosen on the title setup
- Passive. The enemy gathers, builds, and trains. It does not march. It fights if you come to its hall. Line: They fight only if you come to their hall.
- Normal. It attacks if it sees you near its land. It does not hunt across the map. It waits until it has a small army, about five fighters, before it walks out. Line: They strike if they see you, and they do not hunt.
- Aggressive. It comes looking for your hall. It will build a tower. It marches sooner, with about three fighters. Line: They will come looking for your hall.

On every mood the enemy tries to build in this order: barracks, then altar once it has gold to spare, then a pumpkin patch if it is near the cap, then a lodge, then a tower only if the mood is aggressive. It trains up to about five workers, then soldiers, then its hero if the altar is up and the hero is not already alive. Workers gather the nearest gold or wood. If a building is unfinished, idle workers finish it before they go back to gathering. The same return-to-work rule applies to the player's workers.

There is no upkeep. More units do not tax your gold. There is no day-night clock.


============================================================
10. PRICES AND BODY NUMBERS
============================================================

Buildings, repeated so a builder does not have to hunt:
- Pumpkin patch: 40 gold, 70 lumber, about 16 seconds, 280 health, plus 8 cap.
- Barracks: 150 gold, 80 lumber, about 24 seconds, 520 health.
- Altar: 180 gold, 70 lumber, about 26 seconds, 480 health.
- Lodge: 140 gold, 90 lumber, about 24 seconds, 460 health.
- Tower: 100 gold, 50 lumber, about 18 seconds, 420 health.
- Hall raise: 200 gold, 150 lumber. Hall health about 900.

Training
- Worker: 50 gold, about 12 seconds, 1 population. About 46 health, 5 damage.
- Melee: 75 gold, about 15 seconds, 1 population. About 100 health, 10 damage, short reach.
- Ranged: 80 gold, 20 lumber, about 16 seconds, 1 population. About 68 health, 8 damage, longer reach.
- Caster: 90 gold, 25 lumber, about 18 seconds, 1 population. About 62 health, 11 damage, longer reach.
- Hero: 240 gold, 80 lumber, about 28 seconds, 2 population. About 220 health, 16 damage, a little faster than a soldier, starts near 80 mana, can hold about 100.

Faction changes to those numbers
- Vampire health is lower, about four fifths, on every unit including the hero. Their hits heal them a little.
- Ghost move speed is a little higher.
- Werewolf soldiers, not workers, cost about 20 percent more gold, move faster, and hit harder.
- Zombie and mummy units, except the hero, cost about 20 percent less gold and have a little less health.

A worker's carry cap is about 12. Gold swings add about 8. Wood swings add about 10. They must stand in front of the node.


============================================================
11. HERO RULES
============================================================

These are the Warcraft hero rules, rewritten for this game. One hero. Hired only at the altar. If you already have a living hero, the altar will not hire another. The line is: You already have a hero. The altar can revive them.

If the hero dies, it is not deleted. It leaves no corpse for Night Rising. Revive on the altar brings it back with the same level and the same items, if those items were not dropped. Death drops the items on the ground first. Revival costs 160 gold and 40 lumber. If the hero is alive, Revive says: Your hero is still standing.

Levels 1 to 6. That is the cap. Experience comes from kills near the hero. A creep is worth less than an enemy soldier. An enemy hero is worth the most, about 80 experience. An enemy soldier is about 28. A creep is about 18 plus a little for how much health it had. Creeps stop giving experience once the hero is level 5. Each level gives one skill point and about 18 health. The experience steps are about 50, then 110, then 190, then 290, then 420 to reach level 6.

Four abilities. The first three rise to 3 ranks. The fourth is the ultimate. It cannot be learned before level 6, and it has 1 rank. The line if you try early: The ultimate opens at level 6.

Pressing an ability that is not yet learned spends a skill point if you have one and the rank is legal. Pressing it after it is learned casts it. Auras do not cost mana and do not go on cooldown. Their button can say they are always on. Other spells cost mana. If mana is short: Not enough mana. Ordinary spells wait about 9 seconds. The ultimate waits about 28 seconds.

A level 1 hero is stronger than one soldier and weaker than a group of soldiers. Items and levels are how a hero starts to carry a fight.

Six item slots. The same item does not stack in one slot. There are no recipes and no combining. On death, every carried item falls on the ground and can be picked up again.


============================================================
12. THE FOUR HERO KITS
============================================================

These kits used to be a holy knight, a phantom, a blademaster, and a death knight. Only the Halloween versions exist. Do not show the old names anywhere.

Vampire Lord
- Blood Tithe. If a hurt vampire ally is close, heal that ally. The heal is about 28 for each rank. If no ally needs it, wound one living enemy for about 22 for each rank. Costs mana. This is the heal-or-harm spell. It does not heal werewolves, ghosts, or mummies.
- Mist Form. The lord cannot be hurt for a short time, a little longer at higher rank, a bit over two seconds. Costs mana. Attacks against him show mist.
- Blood Aura. Once learned, vampire hits steal a little more life. Always on. No mana. Ranks make the steal larger. This is the devotion that used to be armor. Here it is blood, not plate.
- Night Rising. Level 6 only. Brings two nearby dead allies back at a little over half health. If there is no fresh corpse, the spell does not fire and does not spend mana. Line: No fresh corpses nearby. Costs a large amount of mana. The raised bodies are the units that died, not a new hero.

Ghost Lord
This kit is new. It is not a reskin of a forest faction.
- Wail. Hurts one enemy for about 18 for each rank and slows it for a couple of seconds. Costs mana.
- Fade. Attacks against the lord miss most of the time for a little over two seconds, longer at higher rank. Costs mana.
- Grave Chill. Once learned, nearby enemies strike slower. Always on. No mana. Ranks widen or deepen the chill.
- Procession. Level 6 only. Raises two ghosts that fight for a short time and then fade. They are not corpses afterward. Costs a large amount of mana.

Alpha
- Moon Prowl. He turns to smoke. He can still be ordered. The next hit is much harder, stronger with rank, and then the smoke ends. Costs mana. He is harder to see, but the owner still sees him. Grave Ash and a close enemy can reveal him.
- Savage Strike. Once learned, claws sometimes hit twice as hard. The chance rises with rank. Always on. The button does not cast. Line: Savage Strike is always on.
- Two Shadows. Two pale copies appear beside him, fight for a short time, hit lighter than he does, then fade. Costs mana.
- Wolfstorm. Level 6 only. He spins for about three seconds and hits everyone close. Costs a large amount of mana.

Mummy Lord
- Wrapping Coil. Heals a hurt zombie or mummy ally if one is close, about 26 for each rank. Otherwise wounds an enemy for about 20 for each rank. Costs mana. This is the coil that used to hurt the living and mend the dead.
- Consume. Destroys one nearby zombie worker and heals the lord a large amount, about 90. If none is close, it does not fire. Line: No zombie close enough to consume. Costs mana.
- Rot Aura. Once learned, your zombies and mummies move a little faster while near the lord. Always on. No mana.
- Rise Again. Level 6 only. Raises three zombies for a short time. Costs a large amount of mana.

Mana costs are about 30 for the first spell, 40 for the second, nothing for the aura, and about 70 for the ultimate. Cooldowns are about 9 seconds, or about 28 for the ultimate.


============================================================
13. ITEMS
============================================================

This is the Warcraft item rule, in Halloween materials. Six slots on the hero only. Soldiers do not wear items. No recipes. No stacking. Green is common. Blue is better. Purple is rare. The dropped relic glows that color: green #7dcea0, blue #7eb6e0, purple #c9a0e8.

Materials: pumpkin rind, skull bone, raven feather, bat wing, grave dust, dirty linen, old brass, rusted iron. Not polished plate. Not glowing runeblades from a bright kingdom.

Where they come from
- Clearing a camp drops one item from the strongest body in that camp.
- A green camp drops a green item. An orange camp drops a blue item. The red camp drops a purple item. The Pumpkin King is the body that drops the purple if he dies as the strongest, which he is.
- On hero death, what he carried falls in a small scatter and stays until taken.
- A neutral Grave Peddler stands by one crypt in the middle, not in either base. He is not a building you construct. He sells green items and the cheaper blue items for gold. He does not sell purple. He does not buy items. He does not teach recipes. He does not fight. If you do not want a second building, he is a figure beside a crypt, and tapping him with the hero nearby opens a short list.

Green, common
- Witch's Brew. Drink it once. Heals about 70, then it is gone. A corked bottle of dark red.
- Batwing Boots. Move about 18 percent faster while carried. Black leather, a bat-wing shape at the ankle.
- Raven Talons. A little more damage. A claw on a cord.
- Bone Charm. A finger-bone on a string. A little more health. This is the common jewelry.

Blue, better, and usually permanent
- Specter Gloves. Attack faster. Pale gauze gloves.
- Pumpkin Mace. More damage. A pumpkin head on a haft, the face cut but not on fire.
- Skull Shield. Not a knight's shield. A skull lashed to the forearm. More health, and a small chance a hit is turned aside.
- Grave Ash. Use it once. For about six seconds, hidden things nearby are shown, including a smoked Alpha. Then the pouch is gone. Line: Hidden things are shown.
- Ghoul Heart. More health. A dark red lump in cloth.

Purple, rare, only from the red camp or a boss drop, never from the peddler
- Pumpkin Crown. More health, more mana, and more damage together. A small crown cut from one pumpkin, with a coal glow in the cuts.
- Vampire's Mask. Hits heal the wearer, on top of the vampire blood rule if they are a vampire. A pale mask with red eyes.
- Horseman's Seal. A wax seal with a pumpkin stamp. Once, when the wearer would die, they stay up with about a third of their health. The seal is gone. The word seal floats up.
- Witch Bangle. A thin brass bangle. Spells cost less mana while it is worn.

Tap a brew, the ash, or any other usable item to use it. The rest work by being carried. Selling and combining do not exist.


============================================================
14. CREEPS AND CAMP COLORS
============================================================

Creeps do not build, gather, or use your buildings. They stand in camps and in a few lone places. Their body color is gray-blue so they are not your army and not the enemy faction.

A camp's color is the sum of the levels of the creeps in it. Show that color as a small mark by the camp, or as the glow of the item they will drop. Do not paint the creeps themselves green, orange, and red, or they will look like a new faction.
- Green: the sum is 1 to 9. A few weak bodies. Drops a green item.
- Orange: the sum is 10 to 19. A leader and followers. Drops a blue item.
- Red: the sum is 20 or more. One big camp. Drops a purple item.

Put these camps between the bases:

Green
- Three ghouls.
- Two ravens and one scarecrow.
- Two grave ghosts and one skeleton soldier.

Orange
- One feral werewolf and two grave ghosts.
- One witch on a broom and two feral vampires.
- Three waking trees that are already awake, standing as a grove.

Red
- The Pumpkin King, the Headless Horseman, and one feral vampire.

Creep shapes
- Ghoul. Low, hunched, hungry, claws. Weak melee. A creep, not the player's zombie.
- Grave ghost. A weaker specter. Cold. Slow to strike.
- Scarecrow. Stuffed, a stick arm, a weak swing. Reads as a field thing, not a soldier.
- Raven. Small. Hops or flutters a short distance. Pecks from a little range.
- Skeleton soldier. Thin bones, a rusted sword, melee.
- Skeleton archer. Bones with a bow. Use instead of a raven if a camp needs a clearer ranged shape.
- Feral vampire. A wild vampire, not your knight and not your lord. It bites.
- Feral werewolf. Larger than your brute, smaller than a boss king. Not the Alpha.
- Witch. Hat, broom, purple hex. She stays at a short range.
- Waking tree. A dead tree with eyes and root arms.
- Undead priest. A creep caster in torn wraps. It can mend a hurt creep in its camp a little. It is not the Mummy priest the player trains.
- Warlock. A creep caster. A small purple bolt. It is not a player unit.
- A death knight is not used. The red camp is already the King and the Horseman. Do not add a Warcraft death knight beside them.

Whole camp wakes on one hit. They attack the thing that hit them. They leash back home if you run. They do not respawn. Killing the camp gives a little gold and the one item.

Creeps that are strong, and the red bosses especially, shrug off longer magic. A slow or a hex on the Pumpkin King or the Horseman lasts about half as long, and spells hurt them a little less. The witch would rather hex a soldier than a hero. This is the old rule that high-level creeps shorten spells, kept only on the big bodies.


============================================================
15. BOSS TRICKS
============================================================

Bosses are the same creatures, drawn much larger, with a trick on a cooldown. They are not heroes the player can hire. The Horseman on the title screen is this boss.

Pumpkin King
- A huge body, a pumpkin head, a small flame on top, slow, hard-hitting, a little more reach than a claw.
- Slam, about every four or five seconds. Everyone close is hurt and slowed. The screen shakes once.
- Hurl, when someone is a step farther out. He throws a flaming pumpkin that bursts where it lands. That is the boulder throw, done with a pumpkin.
- While he is under half health, Slam also pulls nearby enemies a step toward him.
- Devour, if a non-hero soldier is standing in his reach when Slam is not ready. He swallows that one soldier, removes it, and heals a bite of health. He cannot devour a hero. He cannot devour a building.
- Spell resistance, as above. Slows and hexes do not last their full time.
- He drops the purple item.

Headless Horseman
- Black horse, headless rider, flaming pumpkin in hand. Faster than the King.
- Charge, about every five seconds. He runs in a line at his target and hurts whoever he passes through. The flaming head can be the thing that flies down the line if a horse charge is hard to read, then it returns to his hand.
- Under half health he runs faster.
- He has the same spell resistance as the King.
- He is not a playable unit.

Feral werewolf, as an orange-camp leader
- Pounce. He leaps onto one target, hurts it, and slows it, then stands and claws.
- Howl. A short ring. Other creeps in his camp move a little faster for a moment, or the pounced target stays slowed. One of those, not a second spell list.

Witch
- Stays on the broom at short range.
- Hex. If the target is not a hero, she slows it and hurts it a little. If it is a hero, she only hurts it. The word hex floats up.

Feral vampire
- Bite. Hurts one target and heals the vampire a little.

Waking tree
- Very slow.
- Slam. A root circle, small, hurts and slows.
- A disguised sleeper becomes this creep when a unit stands on it. The tree tile is then gone. A grove camp is already this creature, three of them.


============================================================
16. MUSIC
============================================================

Original music only. Do not copy a film score and do not copy a game score.

The mood is a quiet Nightmare Before Christmas feeling set next to an old strategy-game menu. Minor. Slow. Low. A little eerie. Not a battle chant and not a dance track. Think a slow waltz in a graveyard: a low pulse, a soft melody on top, room between the notes.

Instruments that fit: a soft bass, a low string, a celesta or a small bell used rarely, a breathy choir-like tone kept quiet. A low horn is allowed if it does not whine. Do not build the loop out of snare, ticking, a metronome, a shaker, or a fast drum. A bass drum is allowed only if it is slow and soft. No high whine. No crackle and no static when the volume slider is at the top. The loop should be long enough that the restart is not obvious every few seconds. It must stay listenable on a phone speaker, so keep the bright edge rolled off.

It starts when the player presses Begin, including on the setup screen, and it continues into the match. Pause does not have to stop it. The music slider does.

Effects are fewer and quieter than the music. A soft tap when an order is given. A hit. A camp waking. They must not click, chirp, or tick in a loop. Gathering sounds, if any, happen on the swing and then stay quiet, they do not machine-gun.


============================================================
17. WORDS ON THE SCREEN
============================================================

Keep every line short.

- Title: Halloweens Bounty.
- Begin.
- Start the night.
- Your faction. Enemy. Enemy mood.
- Vampires. Fragile. Each hit gives a little blood back.
- Ghosts. A little faster. They pass through their own.
- Werewolves. Faster and harder, and they cost more.
- Zombies and Mummies. Cheaper and weaker. The coil heals them.
- They fight only if you come to their hall.
- They strike if they see you, and they do not hunt.
- They will come looking for your hall.
- Not enough gold or lumber.
- You already have a hero. The altar can revive them.
- Your hero is still standing.
- Revival needs 160 gold and 40 lumber.
- Build another pumpkin patch. You are at the unit cap.
- Not enough mana.
- The ultimate opens at level 6.
- No fresh corpses nearby.
- No zombie close enough to consume.
- Savage Strike is always on.
- Raising the hall costs 200 gold and 150 lumber.
- Hidden things are shown.
- The night is yours.
- The hall has fallen.
- Return, back to the title.

The top of the match shows gold, lumber, and population used and cap, a pause button, and the small map. The command bar is the bottom. A floating line can say the last warning, then fade.


============================================================
18. DO NOT ADD
============================================================

Do not add the factions and heroes this game replaced. Do not add a second hero, item recipes, upkeep, a day and night cycle, fog of war, a campaign, portraits, cutscenes, or stick-figure armies. Do not add buildings beyond the town hall, pumpkin patch, barracks, lodge, altar, and tower. Do not add a third resource. Do not let creeps respawn. Do not let the player hire the Horseman, the Pumpkin King, the witch, or the waking trees. Do not put a shield icon on the barracks. Do not give soldiers armor. The Grave Peddler is the only seller, and he is neutral, in the wild, not a player building.
